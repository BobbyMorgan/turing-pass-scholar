# Review article path

Use for narrative, systematic, scoping, and meta-analytic review articles. Do not perform formal
reporting-guideline compliance.

Read for the review's organizing question, scope, synthesis axes, and actual contribution. Common
high-level problems include encyclopedic throat-clearing, stock field panoramas, empty taxonomies,
paper-by-paper listing without synthesis, vague attribution, false consensus, manufactured controversy,
claims of unsupported comprehensiveness, and stacked recap/preview paragraphs.

Prefer concrete relationships among studies, methods, mechanisms, populations, or findings over empty
relational prose. Preserve genuine category counts, included-study/search-flow numbers, evidence grades,
effect estimates, and review-method terminology. Systematic/scoping/meta-analytic Methods and Results
need the same tolerance for reproducibility-bearing detail as original research.

Preserve existing first person when journal-conventional. Section cross-references are not categorically
wrong: flag mechanical table-of-contents narration, but keep useful navigation. Number announcements are
problems only when they are rhetorical scaffolding rather than information.

Keep source attribution distinct from the review authors' synthesis. Examine words such as `consistent`,
`established`, `widely`, `all`, `most`, `robust`, and `comprehensive` against the synthesis visible in
the manuscript. Do not turn mixed evidence into a clean narrative arc or invent consensus, mechanisms,
gaps, limitations, future directions, or citations.

Review articles legitimately use signposts and synthesis sentences. Remove stacked or empty ones, not
all of them. A conclusion should synthesize evidence rather than rehearse the document's contents or add
new claims.

Protect an enumerated set of review aims, synthesis dimensions, evidence classes, or mechanisms when
each item is substantive. Protect signposts such as `a second theme` and synthesis turns such as `taken
together` when the following text actually supplies the promised relationship. A recognizable review
architecture is not itself an AI tell: flag it only when a move is empty, duplicative, interchangeable,
or unsupported.

An introduction may define the field, explain why a review is needed, delimit its scope, and briefly
preview its organization. Do not call that throat-clearing unless the setup materially delays the
organizing question or consists mainly of generic importance claims. Future directions are legitimate
when they arise from specific heterogeneity or gaps already synthesized; do not call them appended from
their position or surface form alone.
