# Academic AI-tell attention guide

Consult this only to test a candidate already noticed during first-pass reading. It is an editor's
attention guide, not a source of candidates, banned list, or diagnostic engine. A device becomes
suspicious through context, concentration, prominence, or emptiness. Diagnose the larger problem when
several symptoms form one paragraph- or section-level pattern; do not search the text to populate these
categories. The relevant genre path overrides this catalog when it protects a legitimate convention.

## Framing and document architecture

- Generic field panoramas, throat-clearing, and claims that a topic is important before saying why.
- Stacked recap, preview, and transition sentences before substantive content.
- Sections built from interchangeable paragraphs, repeated miniature summaries, or a polished coda
  after every subsection.
- A paragraph that adds no fact, comparison, qualification, synthesis, or argumentative turn.

## Formulaic rhetoric

- Empty `not X but Y`, `not merely X`, manufactured conventional-wisdom foils, or unsupported contrast.
- Padded triads, bold First/Second/Third scaffolds, and abstract lists that only re-summarize.
- Rhetorical questions, reader address, dramatic fragments, or instructions telling the reader what to feel.
- Every sentence carrying the same certainty, cadence, and rhetorical weight.

## Mechanical flow

- Consecutive `However`, `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`, `Notably`, or `Importantly` openers.
- Transitions that conceal an unstated relation between claims.
- Clustered em dashes, serial colon setup/elaboration, or semicolons used merely to replace deleted marks.
- Anti-AI repairs that create abrupt stubs or conspicuously plain, rhythmically mechanical prose.

## Inflated abstraction

- Significance and novelty inflation (`pivotal`, `transformative`, `groundbreaking`, `watershed`).
- Generic evaluative vocabulary (`seamless`, `comprehensive`, `robust`, `nuanced`, `underscores`,
  `elucidates`, `facilitates`, `leverages`, `paradigm`) when it substitutes for specific meaning.
- Nominalization shells, empty relational verbs (`play a role`, `stand alongside`, `in the context of`,
  `a range of`), and causal welds that name no mechanism.

Protect genuine technical uses. Statistical robustness, robust control, defined paradigms, measured
leverage, technical lists, proof structure, and field terminology are not AI tells merely because the
surface word appears here.

## Evidence and referents

- `studies show`, `research suggests`, `widely believed`, or citation clusters with unclear attribution.
- Attributed claims rewritten as the current paper's result.
- Synonym cycling for one technical object, drifting labels, unstable abbreviations, unclear pronouns,
  or sudden changes of register/certainty suggesting stitched generation.

Clarify only from supplied material; otherwise recommend author/source verification.

## Generation residue

- `Here is the revised paragraph`, `Certainly`, `as requested`, `I retained the citations`, or other
  assistant-facing text inside the manuscript.
- Markdown scaffolding, alternative phrasings separated by slashes, author instructions, `[insert result]`,
  `<add mechanism>`, `XXX`, `TBD`, and unresolved template slots.

These are strong artifacts, but never guess the missing content.
