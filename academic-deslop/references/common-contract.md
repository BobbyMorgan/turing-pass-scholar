# Common academic editing contract

Apply this contract to both manuscript paths.

## Preserve the scholarship

Before rewriting, notice and preserve all load-bearing content:

- numbers and written-out counts; signs, ranges, units, dates, time points, sample sizes, effects,
  uncertainty, p-values, thresholds, and statistical-test names;
- equations, variables, notation, technical names, group/cohort labels, models, datasets, algorithms,
  accession/sequence identifiers, capitalization, and meaningful hyphenation;
- citations, quotations, DOI/URL strings, and figure/table/equation/section/supplement references;
- LaTeX command names, environments, macros, citation/reference/label keys, math delimiters, and
  BibTeX keys; edit human-readable prose inside headings or captions only when authorized, without
  changing the surrounding syntax;
- negation, direction, comparison, chronology, conditionality, modality, scope, uncertainty,
  attribution, agency, and causal strength;
- author-supplied corrections and established terminology.

Do not normalize a protected token for style. Do not add a fact, citation, method, mechanism, result,
limitation, novelty claim, implication, or recommendation.
Do not invent a convenient status or role label—such as `primary outcome/measure`, `validation set`,
`baseline cohort`, or a named mechanism—when the source does not supply it. Keep an unnamed object
unnamed rather than making the revision sound more complete.

## Use human editorial judgment, not a scanner

Choose the intervention scale by judgment. Local repairs are useful when the problem is local. When a
paragraph's architecture is generic, repetitive, evasive, or logically weak, diagnose the paragraph as
a whole and reconstruct it from its actual claims. When a section has no discernible through-line,
recommend structural revision rather than pretending that word swaps solve it.

Pattern matches are evidence to inspect, not verdicts. Protect literal technical language, quotations,
attributed claims, field conventions, genuine parallelism, and prose that already works. Do not force
an edit merely to demonstrate activity.

The reader has a legitimate aesthetic response. It may say that a passage is clumsy, funny in an
unintended way, pompous, wooden, or simply less effective than it could be, and may suggest a better
version. Do not hide every judgment behind authorial preference. At the same time, keep aesthetic
comments subordinate to de-AI editing and distinguish taste from errors of fact or logic.

For LaTeX sources, do not rewrite command names, environments, macros, labels, citation keys, reference
keys, bibliography commands, or mathematics as prose. Preserve comments and custom commands unless the
user explicitly asks to clean them. A heading or caption inside a command may be edited while its LaTeX
wrapper remains intact.

Tables are read-only: audit them but do not rewrite cells or data. Captions and notes may be edited under
the selected mode, but labels, values, conditions, and abbreviation definitions remain fixed.

## Academic-editorial attention allowed beside de-AI work

Mention only material issues visible during the same read:

- section content does not perform the function promised by its heading;
- objective, Methods, Results, abstract, and conclusion drift in outcome, population/system,
  comparison, direction, or scope;
- association becomes causation, possibility becomes certainty, or a narrow result becomes general;
- another source's statement becomes the current authors' statement;
- numbers, labels, time points, terminology, abbreviations, or figure/table references conflict;
- an antecedent, actor, comparison, or modifier is ambiguous enough to change meaning;
- duplicated draft versions, unresolved placeholders, or a bridge joins claims the source does not join.

Do not expand this into methodology, statistics, ethics, novelty, plagiarism, reporting-guideline, or
journal-acceptance review. Do not silently decide which conflicting claim is correct; ask the author to
reconcile it.

## Rewrite check

After any rewrite, compare it with the source and surrounding section. Verify that facts, tokens,
negation, direction, sequence, attribution, uncertainty, scope, and citation support have not moved.
Check every new causal or degree word (`because`, `therefore`, `only`, `all`, `first`, `most`,
`significant`, `robust`, and similar). Undo any elegant change that makes the scholarship less accurate.
Also inspect every newly introduced noun label or status term and remove it unless the source grounds it.
