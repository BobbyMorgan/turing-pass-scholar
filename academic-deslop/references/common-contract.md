# Common academic editing contract

Apply this contract to both manuscript paths.

## Scientific load-bearing content

Preserve:

- every number, sign, range, unit, date, time point, sample size, effect, uncertainty, p-value,
  threshold, and statistical-test name;
- equations, variables, notation, technical names, group/cohort labels, models, datasets, algorithms,
  identifiers, capitalization, and meaningful hyphenation;
- citations, quotations, DOI/URL strings, and figure, table, equation, section, and supplement
  references;
- negation, direction, comparison, chronology, conditionality, modality, scope, uncertainty,
  attribution, agency, and causal strength;
- established terminology, author-supplied corrections, and the author's stated position.

For LaTeX, preserve commands, environments, macros, labels, citation/reference keys, bibliography
commands, math delimiters, mathematics, comments, and custom commands. Human-readable heading or
caption text may be edited when authorized without changing its wrapper.

Tables are read-only: audit but do not rewrite cells or data. Captions and notes may be edited under
the selected mode while their labels, values, conditions, and abbreviation definitions remain fixed.

Do not add facts, citations, methods, mechanisms, results, limitations, novelty claims, implications,
or recommendations. Do not invent a convenient label such as `primary outcome`, `validation set`,
`baseline cohort`, or a named mechanism when the source leaves the object unnamed.

## Editorial authority

A local defect may receive a local repair; a generic, repetitive, evasive, or logically weak paragraph
may be reconstructed from its actual claims; a section without a through-line may need structural
revision. Protect literal technical language, quotations, attributed claims, field conventions,
genuine parallelism, and prose that already works.

The reader may make a candid aesthetic judgment and propose a better version. Keep taste subordinate
to de-AI editing and distinguish it from errors of fact or logic.

During the same read, mention only material issues such as:

- drift among the objective, Methods, Results, abstract, and conclusion;
- escalation from association to causation, possibility to certainty, or narrow result to general claim;
- another source's statement becoming the current authors' statement;
- conflicting numbers, labels, time points, terminology, abbreviations, or cross-references;
- ambiguity in an actor, antecedent, comparison, modifier, or section function that changes meaning;
- duplicate draft versions, unresolved placeholders, or a bridge joining claims the source does not join.

Do not expand into methodology, statistics, ethics, novelty, plagiarism, reporting-guideline, or
journal-acceptance review. Do not silently decide which conflicting claim is correct.

## Preservation check

After rewriting, compare the result with the source and surrounding section. Verify facts, protected
tokens, negation, direction, sequence, attribution, uncertainty, scope, citation support, and every new
causal or degree word. Remove any new noun label or status not grounded in the source. Undo an elegant
change if it makes the scholarship less accurate.
